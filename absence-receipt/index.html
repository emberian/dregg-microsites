Automata program · demo (a)

Nothing
was there.

A receipt for an absence: one hundred thousand public documents, a two-pattern scanner, and a signed sentence saying it rejected none of them. Zero rejections, not zero secrets. The interesting part is not the zero. It is saying exactly which part of the sentence was proved, which was re-executed, and which was merely hashed.

The sentence.

{"v":"absence-receipt-v1",
 "policy_blake3":"984aba5f…",     the policy source
 "table_blake3":"7434e218…",      the automaton it compiled to
 "corpus_mmr_root":"17d69bbf…",   100,000 documents, in order
 "corpus_pin":"681f94fc…",        crates.io-index commit
 "n":100000,
 "count":0}

The documents are the first 100,000 files of the public crates.io index at a pinned commit · 1.77 GB of package metadata. The policy forbids an AWS access-key id (AKIA and sixteen capitals or digits) and any PEM private-key header. The policy compiles to a 129-state automaton meant to accept exactly the documents containing neither; that is tested, not proved. No claim about other secret formats, encoded secrets, credential validity, or semantic confidentiality.

Which leg is which.

1

Committed. The corpus is a Merkle mountain range over the documents' blake3 hashes. The 100,000 hashes are published, so anyone can recompute the root without downloading the index.

2

Re-executed. The count comes from a Lean program, compiled to native code, that folds the automaton over every byte of every document: 1.77 billion steps in about 48 seconds on a laptop. Lean proves that this fold is the house DFA's acceptance, and that a count of zero means every document is accepted. A second, independent run through the Rust runtime has to agree, and does.

3

Proved. For one 2,048-byte document, a witness generated from that record is emitted by Lean as a descriptor of the served table-routing family and proved as a STARK: 724 ms to prove, 16 ms to verify, 91 KB of proof. The same proof refuses a different final state. What is public is the multiset of transitions, not their order: the proof does not fix a unique word or tie it to that document.

4

Signed. The body is signed with an ordinary SSH key (ssh-keygen -Y sign), and ssh-keygen -Y verify checks it. That authenticates the body under the demo key; it does not attest the checker binary, its execution, or where the scanned bytes came from. To check the claim itself, recompute the corpus root, re-run the table, and compare every field with the signed body.

the zero is cheap.
the labels on the legs are the product.

What this does not claim.

✗

a proof about the corpus · the 100,000 verdicts are re-execution, and checking them means running them again

✗

that the automaton is the policy · the table comes from the runtime crate's Rust compiler, differential-tested against an earlier compiler and a direct scanner, not proved; the Lean-compiled table replaces it when the policy language lands

✗

zero secrets · the scanner looks for two byte patterns; nothing about other secret formats, encoded secrets, whether a match is a live credential, or confidentiality in general

✗

that the hashed documents are the first 100,000 of the pinned commit · re-running the selection against a clone checks that; nothing proves it

✗

a proof about a particular document · the one proof exposes the multiset of its transitions, which is not shown to determine one ordered word, and is tied neither to that document nor to the corpus root; the document's bytes are public anyway

One thing it found.

The runtime crate's own regex-derivative compiler never finished the absence automaton for even one four-letter literal: it normalizes alternation without associativity, so the derivatives of "anything, then AKIA" keep nesting differently and never repeat. Brzozowski's theorem only promises finitely many derivatives up to that similarity, and the Lean side proves exactly the up-to-similarity version. The demo first worked around it (9,153 states, minimized to 129); the crate now keeps its regexes in that normal form and produces the same 129-state table itself, in under three seconds instead of forty.

Status, plainly: a demo, built in a day on public data. The pipeline, the hash list, the table, the receipt and its signature are in the dregg repository under demo/absence-receipt/, with the commands to reproduce every number.
