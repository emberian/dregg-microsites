The host supplies the machine.
Must it also get the ability to
open everything inside?

A machine could learn from what happens to it, change its mind, and answer you—while its running implementation contains no universal reader of its internal state.

That is the target. We have encrypted learners, proof machinery, and narrower cryptographic capabilities. We are working to make them one thing.

01THE REQUIREMENT

A private lifetime.

Encryption usually protects a file until somebody opens it. Here the object has to keep going. Its state changes. New observations arrive. Yesterday’s output becomes part of tomorrow’s world.

“Only learn and infer” is not enough as an API description. Chosen lessons and questions may jointly reveal everything. A copied encrypted snapshot may support a different history. A credential called an encoder may secretly have the power of a master key.

The question is concrete: what can the union of all surviving credentials do? Include old keys, input issuers, archives, evaluation material, and copies. An administrator’s promise is not a cryptographic restriction.

02THE RUNNING PIECES

The learner is real.
The conjunction is open.

A nonlinear encrypted learner has now completed 384 Learn operations and 96 Infer operations. The runtime executes schedules emitted from the Lean description. Independent executions matched all 480 complete ciphertext byte pairs. After the public phase closed, all 484 authorized primary comparisons matched their integer references.

That is an actual encrypted trajectory. It uses a fixed research fixture and retains a full reader key. A separate text path runs a real encoder at a trusted issuer, learns an encrypted contribution, and returns verified answers. Neither experiment makes the encoder private or removes every reader.

A different construction goes after the credentials themselves. Its public setup never constructs a scalar master secret under its stated sampling model. Dedicated recipients can keep computing their fixed projections. A public-seed implementation also runs. But those recipient keys remain usable on retained inputs outside the journal.

The stronger properties currently live in different objects. Joining them means changing the construction—not adding their claims together.

04THE NEXT CONSTRUCTION

The difficult seam
is the useful one.

A release gate must know more than “this looks like an answer.” It must bind the computation to its genesis, parent state, admitted input, recipient, and randomness rule. Otherwise a host can substitute an extraction computation—or use a surviving key to bypass the gate altogether.

There is another arithmetic trap. BFV multiplication includes integer rescaling. Proving a product modulo the ciphertext modulus does not prove the rounded integer result. The relation needs the integer certificate; dropping it can erase exactly the output constraint we wanted.

Today’s construction work pushes several fronts at once: a generated BFV relation reaching an actual prover; fuller convolution and rescale coverage; useful online learning with encrypted updates; an executable ring-based restricted-function scheme; and a bounded transcript/replay route to nonlinear restricted release. These are active attempts, not accomplished results.

The proof system has its own work to do: compose actual folding rounds, move less witness data, and stop recomputing the same arithmetic. Faster proofs matter because the protected learner has to keep living between demonstrations.
